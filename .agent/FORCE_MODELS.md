# Force, torque, and dynamics-model inventory

## Purpose and scope

This is the implementation inventory for force-related spacecraft dynamics in
orskit. It is exhaustive at the model-family level for foreseeable orbital and
attitude work, but no open `ForceModel` trait can make a literally exhaustive
claim: mission-specific forces and empirical accelerations are intentionally
extensible.

The inventory distinguishes physical force families, implementations of those
forces, supporting environment/response submodels, torques, mass flow, frame
terms, discrete state changes, and stochastic corrections. They do not all
belong behind the same evaluation method.

## Force versus model contract

- A **force** names the physical interaction: gravity, aerodynamic force,
  radiation pressure, electromagnetic force, propulsion momentum exchange, or
  another downstream-defined interaction.
- A **force model** is one implementation or approximation of exactly one
  force. Point-mass, spherical-harmonic, irregular-body, time-variable, tidal,
  and relativistic formulations are models of gravity, not separate forces.
- A **supporting submodel/provider** supplies environment or response data to a
  force model. An atmosphere density model, eclipse model, spacecraft panel
  geometry, gravity coefficient source, or thrust profile is not itself a
  force.

`Force` and `ForceModel` are both open object-safe traits. A model reports its
force through `&dyn Force`; `SystemDynamics` stores heterogeneous model trait
objects and therefore never matches on model types, downcasts them, or depends
on a closed model enum.

## Classification contract

The current `ConservativeForceModel` marker should be read as
**potential-derived at a fixed environmental state**. A time-dependent gravity
field, prescribed third-body ephemeris, or rotating tesseral field need not
conserve the spacecraft's mechanical energy even though its instantaneous
acceleration derives from a potential. Conservation tests must therefore name
the closed system and frozen/time-dependent data assumptions they cover.

`NonConservativeForceModel` covers dissipative, radiative, active, empirical,
and other model contributions that are not represented by that potential
contract. Classification belongs to the configured model and its assumptions,
not merely to its force-family name. Some electromagnetic and coupled-body
models require a more specific future classification rather than being forced
into either marker prematurely.

## State and data boundary

A force interaction receives only the propagated spacecraft state. Epoch and
mass are always part of that state; the current dependency descriptor can
additionally declare position, velocity, orientation, and inertia access.
Environmental bodies, ephemerides, frame transforms, field coefficients,
weather, spacecraft surface geometry, optical/aerodynamic coefficients, and
control profiles are immutable model configuration or explicit versioned data
providers.

Several future models reveal state requirements beyond the present first
slice:

- finite thrust, drag, and radiation acceleration require current mass;
- time-varying environments require epoch even if they use no other optional
  state component;
- torque integration requires angular velocity or angular momentum in addition
  to orientation and inertia;
- surface-force torques require shape, centre of mass, centres of pressure, and
  material properties as model configuration;
- propulsion requires mass-flow and often tank, power, or throttle state;
- thermal, charging, flexible-body, and slosh models require additional
  propagated or externally supplied state.

The current state is sufficient to describe inputs to the first translational
models. It is not yet sufficient for coupled six-degree-of-freedom resolution.

## Physical force catalogue

This is the force catalogue. Variants belong in the model inventory below.

| Physical force | Typical origin | Usual classification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gravity | Attraction and spacetime curvature generated by mass-energy | Potential-derived, subject to time-dependent/environment caveats |
| Aerodynamic force | Momentum exchange with a neutral atmosphere, exosphere, coma, plume, gas, or dust | Non-conservative |
| Radiation pressure | Momentum exchange with incident/reflected/emitted photons | Non-conservative |
| Thermal/radiative recoil | Momentum carried away by spacecraft thermal or directed electromagnetic emission | Non-conservative |
| Corpuscular/plasma force | Momentum exchange with solar wind, charged particles, or ambient plasma | Usually non-conservative |
| Electromagnetic force | Interaction of charge, current, electric fields, and magnetic fields | Model-dependent |
| Propulsive force | Deliberate or accidental expelled mass/energy momentum flux | Non-conservative/active |
| Mechanical interaction | Tethers, contact, docking, impact, or coupled participant constraints | Model-dependent; often hybrid rather than an ordinary force model |

Empirical and stochastic accelerations do not identify physical forces. They
are correction models and appear separately below.

## Force-model implementation inventory

### Gravity models

Every row below implements the single physical `GravityForce` family.

| Model implementation | Spacecraft-state dependency | Required configuration/data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Newtonian point mass | Position | Attractor identity and sourced `mu` |
| Multiple point masses | Position | Body ephemerides, sourced `mu` values and frames; third-body differential or absolute N-body assembly is configuration/system policy |
| Static spherical harmonics | Position | Normalised coefficients, degree/order, reference radius and body-fixed transform; includes J2/Jn specialisations and full zonal/tesseral/sectorial fields |
| Irregular-body gravity | Position | Polyhedron/mesh, mascons, ellipsoid or shape harmonics; density/`mu` and body attitude |
| Time-variable gravity coefficients | Position | Secular trends, seasonal/pulsation terms and episodic mass-change series with an explicit convention |
| Solid-body tide modification | Position | Tide-raising ephemerides, Love numbers, frequency convention and body-fixed frame |
| Ocean-tide modification | Position | Ocean-tide coefficients, astronomical arguments, constituent truncation and body-fixed frame |
| Solid/ocean pole-tide modification | Position | Polar motion/EOP and response coefficients |
| Other redistributed-mass gravity | Position | Atmospheric tides, non-tidal ocean/atmosphere, hydrology or ice coefficient series |
| Finite-size/multipole gravity coupling | Position, orientation, inertia/geometry | Spacecraft mass distribution and body gravity derivatives |
| Relativistic gravity | Position, usually velocity | Schwarzschild/first post-Newtonian, Lense-Thirring, de Sitter or configurable PPN/N-body inputs |

Tide-free, zero-tide, and mean-tide are gravity-data conventions, not forces or
standalone force models. Solid, ocean, and pole-tide modifications must not be
double counted against the tide system embedded in a gravity dataset.

### Aerodynamic-force models

| Model implementation | Spacecraft-state dependency | Supporting submodels/data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cannonball/isotropic drag | Position, velocity | Atmosphere density/composition, winds or co-rotation, space weather, area and drag coefficient |
| Panel/tabulated drag | Position, velocity, orientation | Atmosphere provider, spacecraft geometry and surface coefficients |
| Free-molecular, transitional or continuum aerodynamics | Position, velocity, orientation | Regime-specific gas/surface interaction and atmosphere state |
| Lift and side-force model | Position, velocity, orientation | Aerodynamic coefficient/shape model and atmosphere state |
| Exosphere/coma/plume/gas/dust interaction model | Position, velocity, often orientation | Local medium/plume distribution and surface interaction law |

### Radiation-pressure and recoil models

| Model implementation | Spacecraft-state dependency | Supporting submodels/data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct solar cannonball | Position | Solar ephemeris/flux, scalar area and optical coefficient |
| Flat-plate, box-wing/panel or tabulated SRP | Position, orientation | Solar flux plus optical geometry and material coefficients |
| Solar-sail model | Position, orientation | Sail optical/shape model and steering law |
| Planetary albedo/reflected-light pressure | Position, often orientation | Body reflectance/flux map and source/body ephemerides |
| Planetary infrared pressure | Position, often orientation | Body thermal-emission model and optical geometry |
| Spacecraft thermal recoil | Position, orientation and additional thermal state | Thermal power/lag, emissivity and geometry; includes Yarkovsky/Rubincam-like and RTG/equipment effects |
| Directed onboard radiation recoil | Orientation | Antenna, radio, laser or photon-thruster power/direction/efficiency |

Cylindrical/conical umbra and penumbra, extended/multiple occultors, and
self-shadowing are radiation-model modifiers. They are not physical forces.

### Corpuscular, electromagnetic, propulsive, and mechanical models

| Force | Model implementations | Spacecraft-state dependency and configuration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corpuscular/plasma | Solar-wind pressure; Poynting-Robertson/corpuscular drag; ambient plasma interaction | Position, velocity, orientation; particle/plasma flux and interaction coefficients |
| Electromagnetic | Lorentz/electrostatic force; residual dipole; magnetic sail; electrodynamic tether; eddy-current drag | Position, velocity, orientation as applicable; charge/current, field, plasma and hardware models |
| Propulsive | Constant, polynomial, tabulated, feedback or optimised finite thrust; chemical/electric/thermal propulsion; leakage, venting, outgassing, sublimation and boil-off | Orientation and possibly guidance state; thrust/gimbal/throttle, specific impulse, mass flow and triggers |
| Mechanical | Tether tension/damping; coupled formation forces; contact/impact/docking constraint models | Coupled participant state, geometry and material/constraint law |

### Empirical and stochastic correction models

- Constant, piecewise, polynomial, harmonic, once-per-revolution,
  RIC/TNW/body/Sun-frame, ECOM and ECOM2 acceleration models.
- Random walk, Gauss-Markov, dynamic-model compensation and other process-noise
  forcing with explicit seed/realisation and correlation policy.

These models must identify their basis, validity span, estimated parameters and
whether they stand in for a known force. They do not create a new physical
force-family identity merely because they contribute acceleration.

Atmosphere, shadow, radiation flux, spacecraft response, gravity coefficients,
and thrust profiles remain separate supporting submodels/providers. Changing a
density provider must not require a new aerodynamic force type; changing a
box-wing geometry must not redefine solar flux.

## Attitude torques and coupled rigid-body effects

These are not adequately represented by a translational acceleration-only
interface. A future interaction result should be able to contribute force,
torque, and mass rate independently.

| Torque family | Typical variants and dependencies |
| --- | --- |
| Gravity-gradient and higher multipole | Central/extended-body gravity acting on the spacecraft inertia and mass distribution; position, orientation, inertia |
| Aerodynamic | Offset centre of pressure, panel forces, lift/drag moments; position, velocity, orientation, geometry |
| Radiation | Direct SRP, albedo, infrared, thermal and antenna recoil moments; position, orientation, optical/thermal geometry |
| Magnetic/electromagnetic | Residual dipole, hysteresis rods, eddy currents, magnetorquers, charged-body/plasma torque; position, orientation and often angular velocity |
| Active actuators | Reaction wheels, control-moment gyros, thrusters, magnetic torquers and gimballed engines; orientation, angular velocity, actuator state |
| Internal coupled motion | Fuel slosh, flexible appendages, moving mechanisms and rotor gyroscopic coupling; orientation, angular velocity and additional internal state |
| Leakage/impact/contact | Off-centre venting, plume impingement, docking/contact and debris impacts; hybrid event/contact state and geometry |
| Thermal/YORP-like | Delayed anisotropic emission and re-radiation torque; orientation and thermal state |

Internal forces alone do not accelerate the total spacecraft barycentre, but
they can change attitude and alter how external forces act. Propellant expulsion
and radiation emission are external momentum fluxes and therefore can change
translation as well as attitude.

## Terms that should not masquerade as ordinary force models

- **Non-inertial-frame terms:** translational frame acceleration, centrifugal,
  Coriolis, and Euler acceleration belong to frame-aware equation assembly.
- **Impulsive manoeuvres, staging, deployment, collision, and docking:** these
  are discrete or constrained state transitions, not finite continuous forces.
- **CR3BP, elliptic restricted three-body, Hill/Clohessy-Wiltshire, and other
  reduced systems:** these are complete dynamical formulations/approximations,
  not individual forces to compose blindly with a full model.
- **Ephemeris interpolation and prescribed trajectories:** these provide
  environmental state; they are not forces.
- **Process noise:** this is an estimation/dynamics uncertainty model even when
  represented numerically as a stochastic acceleration.

## Suggested implementation order

1. Point-mass and third-body attraction, J2 then full spherical harmonics.
2. Cannonball drag with a replaceable atmosphere/wind provider.
3. Cannonball solar radiation pressure with explicit eclipse geometry.
4. Finite manoeuvres with mass flow and discrete impulsive manoeuvres.
5. Relativity and solid/ocean/pole tides with versioned data conventions.
6. Attitude-dependent panel drag/SRP, albedo and planetary infrared.
7. Empirical accelerations and estimation parameters.
8. Coupled torque dynamics, then specialised thermal, electromagnetic,
   irregular-body, plume, tether, and flexible/slosh models as missions demand.

The order is about establishing reusable contracts and validation evidence; it
does not imply that later effects are always physically smaller.
